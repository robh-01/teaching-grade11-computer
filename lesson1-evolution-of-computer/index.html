Abacus

The first computer

Abacus

~4000 years ago
  • Abacus is believed to be the first computer.
  • It originated around 4,000 years ago.
  • It was invented by Chinese people.
  • It was used for arithmetic calculations.
  • Abacus is still used in some countries.

Structure of an Abacus

Parts
  • An abacus consists of a wooden frame.
  • Metal rods hold movable beads.
  • A central bar separates the beads into two sections.
  • The upper section is called heaven.
  • The lower section is called earth.

Abacus Beads

Values
  • Each upper section contains two beads.
  • Each upper bead represents five units.
  • Each lower section contains five beads.
  • Each lower bead represents one unit.
  • Beads are moved according to rules to perform calculations.

Abacus Place Value

Columns
  • Each column represents a place value.
  • The rightmost column represents ones.
  • The next column represents tens.
  • The next represents hundreds.
  • Larger columns represent thousands, millions, and so on.

Abacus Today

Still in use
  • Abacus is not only a historical device.
  • It is still used in many countries.
  • Examples include:
    • China
    • Russia
    • Japan
Fun fact · Abacus is still used in many countries.

Napier's Bones

John Napier · 1617

Napier's Bones

1617
  • Invented in 1617 AD.
  • Invented by Scottish mathematician John Napier.
  • It was a calculating device based on logarithms.
  • It consisted of 11 bone rods.

How Napier's Bones Worked

Logarithms
  • Logarithms allow multiplication to be performed through addition.
  • Napier's Bones used this principle.
  • The rods contained numerical values.
  • The first horizontal rod starts from 1 to 0.
  • The first vertical rod starts from 1 to 9.
  • Multiplication was performed using addition of logarithmic values.

Slide Rule

William Oughtred · 1620

Slide Rule

1620
  • Invented in 1620 AD.
  • Invented by English mathematician William Oughtred.
  • It was based on the principle of logarithms.
  • It was developed after the invention of logarithms in 1617.

Structure of Slide Rule

Two rulers
  • A slide rule contains marked scales.
  • It has an inner ruler.
  • It has an outer ruler.
  • The inner ruler can be shifted in and out.
  • The scales are aligned to perform calculations.

Uses of Slide Rule

Alignment
  • Slide rule was used for calculating:
    • Products
    • Quotients
  • Calculations were performed by properly aligning two scales.
  • It made logarithmic calculations practical.

Pascaline

Blaise Pascal · 1642–1644

Pascal's Calculating/Adding Machine

1642–1644
  • Also called:
    • Arithmetic Machine
    • Adding Machine
    • Pascaline
  • Developed between 1642 and 1644.
  • Invented by French mathematician-philosopher Blaise Pascal.
  • It is considered the first mechanical and automatic calculator.

How Pascaline Worked

Mechanics
  • Pascal invented the machine to help his father.
  • His father was a tax accountant.
  • Pascaline could perform:
    • Addition
    • Subtraction
  • It was made as a wooden box.
  • It contained gears and wheels.

Pascaline's Gears

Wheels & windows
  • The machine used interconnected wheels.
  • Rotating one wheel one revolution moved the neighboring wheel.
  • A series of windows was placed above the wheels.
  • The windows displayed the calculated totals.

Stepped Reckoner

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibnitz · 1673

Stepped Reckoner / Leibnitz Wheel

1673
  • Developed in 1673 AD.
  • Developed by German mathematician-philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibnitz.
  • Leibnitz improved Pascal's invention.
  • It was a digital mechanical calculator.
  • It was called the Stepped Reckoner.

Fluted Drums

Mechanism
  • The Stepped Reckoner used fluted drums.
  • It used these instead of the gears used in Pascal's machine.
  • The fluted drums enabled mechanical calculation.

1830

ENIAC

Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer

ENIAC

1942
  • ENIAC stands for Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer.
  • Developed in 1942 AD.
  • It was an early electronic computer.
  • It used thousands of electronic components.
  • It occupied about 1,800 square feet.

ENIAC Specifications

By the numbers
  • Approximately 20,000 vacuum tubes.
  • Approximately 10,000 capacitors.
  • Approximately 70,000 resistors.
  • Used about 200 kilowatts of electricity.
  • Weighed more than 30 tons.
  • Cost about $487,000.

EDVAC

Electronic Discrete Variable Automatic Computer

EDVAC

1946
  • EDVAC stands for Electronic Discrete Variable Automatic Computer.
  • Developed around 1946 AD.
  • It was developed after ENIAC.
  • ENIAC required physical changes to its wiring to change programs.
  • Reprogramming ENIAC could take days.

Stored Program Concept

Program + Data → Memory
  • EDVAC introduced the stored-program approach.
  • Programs could be represented electronically.
  • Programs could be stored along with data.
  • This reduced the need for physical rewiring.
  • The stored-program concept became a major breakthrough in computer development.

John von Neumann and Stored Programs

Breakthrough
  • John von Neumann is often associated with the stored-program concept.
  • He published a description of the concept.
  • Earlier work on the idea existed in Eckert's notes.
  • The breakthrough allowed computation steps to be represented electronically.

EDSAC

Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator

EDSAC

1949
  • EDSAC stands for Electronic Delay Storage Automatic Calculator.
  • Developed in Britain.
  • It was created at the University of Cambridge.
  • It was an early stored-program electronic computer.
  • Its first calculation was performed on 6 May 1949.

EDSAC and OXO

Early gaming
  • EDSAC has an important place in computer-game history.
  • It ran OXO.
  • OXO was an implementation of tic-tac-toe.
  • It is associated with one of the earliest graphical computer games.

UNIVAC

Universal Automatic Computer

UNIVAC

1950
  • UNIVAC stands for Universal Automatic Computer.
  • Developed around 1950 AD.
  • It was a general-purpose electronic digital computer.
  • It was designed for business applications.
  • It was produced in the United States.
  • It was developed by J. Presper Eckert and John Mauchly.

UNIVAC and Commercial Computing

First commercial
  • By the end of the 1950s, computers were no longer only unique research machines.
  • Computers began being commercially produced.
  • UNIVAC became a famous commercial computer.
  • The first UNIVAC was sold to the U.S. Census Bureau.
  • UNIVAC was the first computer to employ magnetic tape.
  • It became the first commercially used electronic computer in the world.

Generations of Computers

Chapter — Classifying by technology

What Is a Computer Generation?

Classification
  • Computer generations classify computers according to their technology.
  • Classification considers:
    • Hardware
    • Memory devices
    • Software
  • Each generation is characterized by a major technological development.
  • New technology changed how computers operated.
  • Computers became increasingly:
    • Smaller
    • Cheaper
    • More powerful
    • More efficient
    • More reliable

First Generation: Overview

Recap
  • First generation: 1940–1956 AD.
  • Main technology: vacuum tubes.
  • Computers were large and expensive.
  • Computers were slow compared with later generations.
  • They produced significant heat.
  • They relied heavily on punch cards, magnetic tape and paper tape.
