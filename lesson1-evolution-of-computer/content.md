# Abacus

## Slide 4 — Abacus

**Bullet points**

* Abacus is believed to be the first computer.
* It originated around 4,000 years ago.
* It was invented by Chinese people.
* It was used for arithmetic calculations.
* Abacus is still used in some countries.

**Image:** Photograph/illustration of a traditional Chinese abacus.

---

## Slide 5 — Structure of an Abacus

**Bullet points**

* An abacus consists of a wooden frame.
* Metal rods hold movable beads.
* A central bar separates the beads into two sections.
* The upper section is called **heaven**.
* The lower section is called **earth**.

**Image:** Simple labeled abacus diagram.

---

## Slide 6 — Abacus Beads

**Bullet points**

* Each upper section contains two beads.
* Each upper bead represents five units.
* Each lower section contains five beads.
* Each lower bead represents one unit.
* Beads are moved according to rules to perform calculations.

**Image:** Close-up of an abacus showing upper and lower beads.

---

## Slide 7 — Abacus Place Value

**Bullet points**

* Each column represents a place value.
* The rightmost column represents ones.
* The next column represents tens.
* The next represents hundreds.
* Larger columns represent thousands, millions, and so on.

**Image:** Abacus with place-value labels above the columns.

---

## Slide 8 — Reading an Abacus

**Bullet points**

* Beads pushed against the central bar represent the final number.
* Numbers are read from left to right.
* A five-unit bead represents **5**.
* A one-unit bead represents **1**.
* Combining beads gives the value of a column.

**Image:** Small annotated abacus showing one example.

---

## Slide 9 — Abacus Example

**Bullet points**

* A five-unit bead + two one-unit beads = 7.
* If these beads are in the hundreds column:
* 7 × 100 = 700.
* Therefore, the column represents **700**.

**Image:** Zoomed-in hundreds column showing 5 + 1 + 1.

---

## Slide 10 — Abacus Today

* It is still used in many countries.
* Examples include:

  * China

# Napier's Bones

## Slide 11 — Napier's Bones

**Bullet points**

* Invented in **1617 AD**.
* Invented by Scottish mathematician **John Napier**.
* It was a calculating device based on logarithms.
* It consisted of **11 bone rods**.

**Image:** Historical illustration of Napier's Bones.

---

## Slide 12 — How Napier's Bones Worked

**Bullet points**

* Logarithms allow multiplication to be performed through addition.
* Napier's Bones used this principle.
* The rods contained numerical values.
* The first horizontal rod starts from 1 to 0.
* The first vertical rod starts from 1 to 9.
* Multiplication was performed using addition of logarithmic values.

**Image:** Napier's Bones diagram.

### One important presentation principle

Don't put paragraphs from the textbook onto the slides. The **slides should contain the atomic facts**, while **your explanation happens verbally**.

For example, instead of:

> "The Difference Engine was a steam driven calculating machine designed to solve tables of numbers like logarithm tables..."

Use:

**Difference Engine**

* Designed by Charles Babbage
* Early 1820s
* Mechanical computer
* Steam-driven
* Performed calculations
* Designed to produce numerical tables
* Example: logarithm tables
